# Food Business Success Prediction Model – Performance Review

## Dataset Summary

* **Total Records:** 1,856
* **Features Used:** 10
* **Target Variable:** `success_label`
* **Class Distribution:**

  * Successful Businesses: **427 (23.01%)**
  * Unsuccessful Businesses: **1,429 (76.99%)**

To address the class imbalance, **SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique)** was applied only to the training dataset before model training.

---

# Machine Learning Model

* **Algorithm:** Random Forest Classifier
* **Number of Trees:** 350
* **Maximum Depth:** 12
* **Minimum Samples Split:** 4
* **Minimum Samples Leaf:** 3
* **Class Weight:** Balanced
* **Feature Scaling:** StandardScaler
* **Train-Test Split:** 75% Training, 25% Testing
* **Cross Validation:** 5-Fold

---

# Model Performance

## Test Accuracy

**93.75%**

The model correctly classified **93.75%** of unseen test samples.

---

## Cross Validation

**92.56% ± 1.43%**

The low standard deviation indicates that the model performs consistently across different data splits and is unlikely to be overfitted to a single train-test partition.

# Key Findings

The model indicates that the most influential factors affecting business success are:

* High customer footfall
* Strong office presence
* Higher purchasing power (average income)
* Commercial rent levels
* Nearby restaurant activity

Less influential features in the current dataset include nearby parks, shops, and hospitals.

---

# Strengths

* High overall prediction accuracy (93.75%)
* Strong cross-validation performance (92.56%)
* Good generalization across unseen data
* Handles imbalanced classes using SMOTE
* Uses real-world location, demographic, and economic features
* Suitable for deployment as the prediction engine of the Food Business Prediction application

---

# Current Limitations

* Success labels are generated using a rule-based scoring approach rather than verified historical business outcomes.
* The current model is trained using data collected from Hyderabad only.
* Predictions should be interpreted as **business potential estimates**, not guarantees of commercial success.

---

# Conclusion

The Random Forest model demonstrates strong predictive performance and stable generalization, making it suitable as the Version 1 prediction engine for the Food Business Prediction platform. With an accuracy of **93.75%** and consistent cross-validation performance, the model provides a solid foundation for evaluating business location potential using geospatial, demographic, and commercial features.
